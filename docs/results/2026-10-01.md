# Sweep of 2026-10-01: seven local models and two coding agents

Machine: AMD Radeon RX 7900 XT (20 GiB, about 13 GiB free with the desktop running), Ryzen 9
9950X, 64 GB RAM, Windows 11. llama.cpp build b11321, Vulkan. hrn 0.2.0. Sweep file:
[`sweeps/local-7900xt.json`](../../sweeps/local-7900xt.json); hrn-evals at the `gobench-suite`
branch (cristiangirlea/hrn-evals#1). Each task ran 3 times per target: 21 attempts per target,
189 in all.

Every local model ran with 4 slots of 24k context and an 8-bit KV cache. Speed: 256-token
completions, thinking off, one request and then four at once. Evals: hrn's own agent loop
(`backend: api`) against llama-server's Anthropic API; the agents through their CLIs.

The full sweep ran gpt-oss-20b with 8 expert layers on the CPU, a guess: 45 t/s decode, 80 t/s
total, 13m24s. Measured afterwards, the model fits on the GPU entirely and is 3 to 4 times faster
there (see below), so it was re-run alone with no layers on the CPU; its row is the re-run,
merged with `bench report`. Its pass count was 20/21 both times.

## Reading it

- **Ornith-1.5-9B**, an agentic-coding fine-tune of Qwen3.5-9B, passed every attempt, its base
  model 19/21. The fine-tune's claim of a large gain over its base agrees with this, on a small
  sample.
- **The larger local models gained nothing on these tasks**: gpt-oss-20b and Gemma 4 26B-A4B
  passed 20/21, like Granite 4.2 8B. The tasks are small; they do not stretch a larger model.
- **gpt-oss-20b, tuned, is the fastest decoder** (164 t/s, about 0.15 s to first token) and among
  the most accurate. Gemma 4 26B-A4B is the slowest because it does not fit on the GPU and runs 12
  expert layers on the CPU.
- **Claude Sonnet** passed 20/21 for $3.24; its one miss was an explanation of 309 words against
  a limit of 300. **Codex** passed 21/21; the Codex CLI reports one turn per run and no cost, so
  its turns and cost columns are not comparable.
- **Qwen3.5-4B** is fast but unreliable: 2 of 3 attempts at the trivial greeting task failed, and
  one Fibonacci attempt grew past the 24k context per slot.
- One attempt per task would have ranked these models differently: the 4B model passed the
  greeting task in a single-attempt run earlier the same day. Use `evals.repeats`.

As a server (4 slots, 96k context, q8_0 KV), no layers on the CPU: 168 t/s for one request, 305
t/s total for four, VRAM 18.4 GiB dedicated with shared memory at the desktop's own 2.5 GiB, so
nothing spills. Offloading pays only for a model that does not fit; for one that fits, every
layer moved to the CPU costs speed.
